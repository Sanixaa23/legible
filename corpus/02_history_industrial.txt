The Industrial Revolution did not begin everywhere at once, and historians generally date its earliest phase in Britain to roughly 1760, although the changes it describes had been gathering for decades before that in agriculture, in trade and in the organisation of work.

Between 1750 and 1850 the population of Britain grew from about 6 million to nearly 21 million, and most of that increase was absorbed by towns, so that by 1851 slightly more than half the population lived in an urban area for the first time in the country's recorded history.

Working conditions in the new mills were often dangerous, and although the Factory Act of 1833 limited the working day for children under 13 to eight hours, enforcement depended on only four inspectors for the whole country, which meant the law was frequently ignored.
