About 97 percent of the water on Earth is in the oceans and is too salty to drink or to use for most crops, and of the roughly 3 percent that is fresh, more than two thirds is locked in ice sheets and glaciers, so less than 1 percent of all the water on the planet is available as liquid fresh water.

The water cycle moves water between these stores. Water evaporates from oceans and lakes, transpires from plants, condenses into clouds, falls as precipitation, and returns to the sea through rivers and through groundwater, and the whole cycle is driven by energy from the Sun.

Groundwater can take a very long time to replenish. An aquifer that is pumped faster than rain can refill it will fall year after year, and in some regions the water now being extracted fell as rain thousands of years ago, which means it cannot sensibly be called renewable.
